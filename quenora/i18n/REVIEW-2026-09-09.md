# Translation review — German and French

Written 9 September 2026, when German and French were switched back on and
coverage went from 94% to 100%. **Every entry below was written by Claude and
has not been checked by a native speaker.** Everything not listed here was
already in the dictionaries and is not part of this review.

The site is formal throughout: **Sie** in German, **vous** in French.
Terminology follows what the dictionaries already used — DE *Leistungsumfang,
Übergabe, Evaluations-Harness, schriftliche Position, Referenzwahrheit,
Aktualität, Fallstudien, Tagessatz*; FR *périmètre, transfert, harnais
d’évaluation, position écrite, vérité terrain, fraîcheur, études de cas, taux
journalier*. If you change a term, change it everywhere.

## How to read this

Short fragments are halves of a heading split by an `<em>`. They have to read
as one sentence when joined, so a fragment that looks odd alone may be right in
place — check it on the page, not in this list. The pages are `/de/...` and
`/fr/...`.

## Known rough edges, already spotted

- The German nav labels wrap onto two lines in the header — German is longer
  than English and the bar is tight. Working, but worth a look.
- A stray space before a full stop on `de/contact.html` and `fr/work.html`,
  inherited from older entries, not from this pass.
- `de/engineering.html` h1 reads "Sie bleiben stehen an fünf Ebenen"; "Sie
  bleiben an fünf Ebenen stehen" is the natural order. Pre-existing.
