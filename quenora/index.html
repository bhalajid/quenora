The demo was never the hard part.

Nobody’s AI programme fails because the model was weak. It fails in the gap between a notebook and a Tuesday afternoon — where the data will not join, the permissions do not exist, and nobody owns the result.

02

Three situations we are actually called into.

Not a market segment. The three shapes of problem that land on us most weeks. If none of them describe you, we are probably not the right firm — and we would rather you knew that now.

Situation 01

The pilot that stalled

It worked in the demo. Six months later it is still a demo, because the platform underneath it was never built and nobody owns the gap.

Situation 02

Retrieval that lies

The assistant answers confidently and is wrong often enough that people stopped trusting it. The model is fine. The data contract is not.

Situation 03

Governance arriving late

Something is live, procurement or risk is now asking questions, and the evidence to answer them was never captured.

03

Six phases. Designed to end.

Most consultancies are structured to extend the engagement. This one is structured to finish it — every phase carries an exit condition written into the statement of work.

Phase 01 · Frame

Find the real constraint

We spend the first weeks understanding what actually happens, not what the process documentation says happens. Almost every engagement uncovers something that was never in the brief.

  • Process walkthrough with the people doing the work
  • Data and systems inventory
  • The constraint nobody mentioned
Exit conditionA written position on what to build first — or a recommendation to build nothing.

Weeks 1–2

Phase 02 · Foundation

The unglamorous layer

Environments, data contracts, access model, evaluation harness. The work that decides whether anything after it survives contact with production.

  • Environments and access model
  • Data contracts, with owners named
  • Evaluation harness and baseline scores
Exit conditionBaseline evaluation scores you can hold every later change against.

Weeks 3–6

Phase 03 · Integrate

Into what you already run

Connected to the systems that hold the data and run the work, with the failure paths designed rather than discovered in production.

  • Connectors into ERP, CRM, service desk
  • Permission model and audit trail
  • Designed failure and rollback paths
Exit conditionA staged rollout running against real data in a non-production environment.

Weeks 4–10

Phase 04 · Ship

Under real change control

Into production behind the same governance as everything else you run. No parallel universe. No permanent pilot.

  • Release behind your existing change control
  • Monitoring, alerting, cost ceilings
  • Rollback rehearsed, not theorised
Exit conditionIn production, under the same governance as everything else you run.

Continuous

Phase 05 · Prove

Against the agreed number

Measured against the figure set in Frame. If it did not move, we say so, and we say why. That conversation is the product.

  • Measurement against the Frame number
  • What moved, what did not, and why
  • Honest recommendation on continuing
Exit conditionA written result — including the uncomfortable version if that is the truth.

Weeks 9–12

Phase 06 · Hand over

And then we leave

Runbooks, monitoring, paired operation, and a date after which your team runs it without us. The engagement is designed to end.

  • Runbooks and architecture decision records
  • Paired operation with your engineers
  • Named owner and a handover date
Exit conditionYour team runs it without us. That is the definition of done.

Weeks 12–14

05

The questions you are already thinking.

We are a small firm selling into organisations that usually buy from large ones. These are the objections that come up in every first call, answered here so the call can start further along.

Why not a large consultancy?
You should use one for a multi-year, multi-country programme with a thousand stakeholders — they are built for that and we are not. For a single system that needs to reach production this year, their engagement model costs more than the problem and moves slower than your business does. The people who sell it are also rarely the people who build it.
Why not build it in-house?
Often you should, and we will say so. Bring us in when your team has the domain knowledge but not the platform experience, or when they are already fully committed to the systems that keep the business running. The handover phase exists precisely so that in-house becomes the answer at the end.
Why not just buy a vendor platform?
If a product genuinely covers your case, buy it — that is cheaper than anything we would build. The work we get called for is what remains: connecting that product to systems it was never designed to meet, and producing the governance evidence the vendor does not.
You have no published case studies.
Correct, and we will not invent them. Quenora is a founder-led firm in its early chapter. What we will do is arrange a direct conversation with a reference before you commit, and show you the actual architecture and evaluation approach in the first meeting rather than a capability deck.
What happens if it does not work?
Phase 05 exists to answer that in writing, measured against the number agreed in Phase 01. If it did not move we say so. You keep the code, the documentation and the evaluation harness regardless — there is no scenario where you end an engagement with nothing to show for it.
